How to find and join a good clan in Pet Simulator 99

The right clan decides which rewards you get from every clan battle. A top clan can mean a Titanic pet each battle, but only if you can keep up with its requirements. This guide shows how to use the public stats to find a clan that matches how much you actually play.

1. Decide what you want

Be honest about your play time before you look at the list. Clans tend to fall into three groups:

  • Top competitive clans (roughly the top 10) aim for first place. They usually expect a high number of points every battle, often a fixed minimum, and replace members who fall short. Huge rewards, but real pressure.
  • Mid-table clans (around places 10 to 100) aim for a medal tier. Requirements are lower, and a steady player who contributes every day is very welcome.
  • Casual and community clans play together, help each other and still collect participation rewards, with little or no pressure.

Neither choice is wrong. A clan where you are an average member is often more fun and more rewarding over time than one where you are always at the bottom.

2. Make a shortlist from the leaderboard

Open Top Clans during a battle and look at the band you are aiming for.

  • Filter by country with the arrow on the right side of the window. Clans from your region play in your time zone and often speak your language, which makes coordinating pushes much easier.
  • Check the member count. A clan at 50/50 is full. A clan with a few free slots, or one that just bought new ones, is likely recruiting.
  • Switch to diamonds with the gem toggle. A clan that is high on diamonds but lower on points has a strong history, so it may be rebuilding and happy to take new active players.

Pick three to five clans for the next step.

3. Check each clan's profile

Click a clan to open its profile and look at these things:

  1. How many members actually score. Scroll the member list. If most members have points, the clan is active. If only a few people carry the total, you'll be expected to carry too.
  2. What a typical member scores. Look at the members in the middle of the list, not the leader. That's the level you'll be compared with.
  3. The shout. The status message often states the rules ("min 50M per battle", "join our Discord"). A shout posted recently means the leaders are online.
  4. The trend. Use Compare against a neighbour to see if the clan is gaining ground or slowly falling back.

Then check yourself on Top Players. If your points this battle would place you around the middle of a clan's member list, that clan is a realistic fit.

4. Red flags

  • Requests for your items, Robux or account details "to join". No real clan needs them.
  • Many members with zero points late in a battle. The clan may be inactive or about to fall apart.
  • No shout and no description at all. That often means absent leaders.
  • Promises of guaranteed rewards. Nobody can guarantee a final place.
SafetyNever share your password or a login code with anyone, and only trade inside the game's trading system. A clan invite is always free.

5. How to get an invite

Only the leader and officers can invite players. The usual ways in are:

  • the clan's Discord server, if it is linked in the description or shout,
  • asking in the game's trading plaza or in community servers where clans recruit,
  • simply being active and helpful around members of the clan you like.

When you ask, mention your points from the current or last battle. Recruiters like numbers, and you can show them your own profile from Top Players.

6. Staying in a good clan

Once you're in, the simplest way to stay is to be predictable: play the battle event a little every day, deposit diamonds when you can, and tell the officers if you'll be away. During battles clans can only kick a limited number of players per day, so most cuts happen between battles, based on the last battle's contribution.
