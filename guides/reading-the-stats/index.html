How to read PS99 clan and player stats

Updated Oct 29, 20264 min readBy PS99 Clans

A leaderboard shows who is ahead right now. Good decisions need a bit more: how fast each clan is moving, how deep its roster is and how much time is left. This guide explains every number you'll see on PS99 Clans and how to turn them into answers.

Battle points vs diamonds

Two different numbers appear on most clan cards:

  • Battle points (the star icon) only count the current clan battle. They decide this battle's rewards and reset when the next battle begins.
  • Diamonds (the gem icon) are what members have deposited into the clan bank over the clan's whole life. They pay for upgrades and slots and never reset.

So a clan can be first on diamonds and 40th on points. That usually means an old, rich clan in a quiet phase. The opposite, a young clan high on points but low on diamonds, often means a new team of very active players.

Pace: points per hour

Pace is the most important number in a race. A clan that is 5% behind but 20% faster will pass the leader before the end of a long battle. You can see pace in several places:

  • the +X/h value on the Clan Points overlay,
  • the slope of the line on the live tracker graph,
  • the slope of the difference line in Compare, which shows the pace difference directly,
  • the hourly bars and the average line on each player profile.

Pace changes a lot over a day. Evenings in a clan's main time zone are busy and nights are quiet. Judge pace over at least an hour, and compare the same times of day if you can.

Average per member

Total points divided by member count gives the average per member, shown as Avg / person in Compare. It answers a different question than the total: how active is a typical member?

A clan with 35 members and a high average has room to grow by filling its empty slots. A full clan of 50 with a low average depends on a few strong players. If one of them stops, its pace drops sharply. Both kinds of clan can sit at the same place on the leaderboard, but they behave very differently over the rest of a battle.

Gaps and overtake time

To estimate if and when one clan will pass another:

  1. Write down the gap between the two clans (Compare shows it as the point difference).
  2. Note how much the gap changes over one hour. That's the closing speed.
  3. Divide the gap by the closing speed. The result is roughly the number of hours until an overtake.
  4. Compare that with the time left on the battle countdown.

Example: a gap of 2.4B that shrinks by 300M per hour closes in about 8 hours. With 20 hours left, the chasing clan is on track. With 5 hours left, it needs a push.

CarefulThis is a straight-line estimate. Pushes, boosts and time-of-day swings bend the line, so redo the calculation every few hours, and especially on the last day.

Rank changes

Ranks near the top move rarely, because the gaps between the best clans are large. Further down, many clans sit close together and ranks can shuffle every few minutes. When you see a rank badge bounce on an overlay or a card slide on the Live Top 500, that's a real overtake in the latest data.

For players, the global rank on Top Players is calculated among the top 25,000 tracked players. The percentage of players ahead and behind tells you how close you are to the next big milestone, for example the top 1% or the top 1,000.

How fresh is the data?

All numbers come from the official PS99 public API. Refresh times differ by page:

PageRefreshes about every
Clan profile, live tracker, Compare, overlays10 – 15 seconds
Top Clans, Leagues, Live Top 50015 – 60 seconds
Top PlayersA few minutes. Every ranked clan is re-read in a rolling cycle.
Player hourly chartEvery hour gets a new bar. The current hour keeps growing.

When points arrive, the counters roll up over the next seconds instead of jumping. This makes the numbers easier to follow, but it means a counter can briefly show a little less than the official total. During very busy moments, like the final hour of a battle, the official API itself can be a minute or two behind the game.

A worked example

Say your clan is 6th, and the bronze tier ends at 5th. The clan in 5th is 1.8B ahead and the battle ends in 30 hours.

  1. In Compare, the gap shrinks by 90M in an hour in the afternoon, and by 40M in an hour in the evening, when the rival's region is online.
  2. That is about 60M per hour on average, so roughly 30 hours to close 1.8B. That's right on the edge.
  3. The rival is weakest in your afternoon. A planned push then, plus filling your three empty member slots, turns a coin-flip into a likely overtake.

That's the whole idea of this site: the same numbers you could see in game, arranged so you can read the race instead of guessing it.
