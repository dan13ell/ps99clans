How clan battles work in Pet Simulator 99

Updated Oct 29, 20265 min readBy PS99 Clans

Clan battles are the main competitive mode of Pet Simulator 99. Every member of a clan earns points for the same team, and when the timer runs out the clans with the highest totals win huge pets, medals and other limited rewards. This guide explains how a battle works from the first minute to the final reward, and how to follow one on this site.

The basics

Clan battles have been part of Pet Simulator 99 since Update 2, shortly after the game launched. A battle is always tied to an in-game event. During the event, one specific activity earns battle points, and every point a member earns is added to their clan's total. Clans are ranked by that total on a global leaderboard.

Most battles last around one week. Some have run for two or even three weeks, especially in the game's first year. The start and end times are fixed in advance, which is why this site can show an exact countdown on the Top Clans banner.

You need to be in a clan to take part. Points you earn while you are not in a clan don't count for anyone, and points stay with the clan you earned them for. That's why players who switch clans during a battle can't take their score with them.

How points are earned

The scoring activity changes every battle, and that's what keeps the mode fresh. A few examples from past battles show how different they can be:

  • Opening items: in the 2023 Festive battle, presents were worth different points by size, from 1 point for a small present up to 850 for a titanic one.
  • Hatching: the New Year's battle at the turn of 2024 gave 1 point per active huge pet hatched. Several 2025 battles scored rare pets hatched or eggs hatched.
  • Collecting: the Fragmented battle gave 10 points per prison key and 100 per golden key.
  • Minigames: battles have counted Chest Raid rooms, Clown's Millionaire Race rooms, tower height, the farthest basketball throw, muscle gained in a race and progress in Tower Defense.
  • Records: some events ranked the heaviest fish caught or the heaviest pet hatched.

Because the unit changes, raw totals are not comparable between battles. Ten billion points can be a winning total in one battle and a mid-table score in the next. What matters is how a clan compares to the other clans in the same battle.

TipAt the start of each battle, read the event description in game carefully and work out which action gives the most points per minute. The first hours decide which clans settle into the top spots.

The timeline of a battle

Before the start

The next battle is usually announced with the update that brings its event. Clans use this time to recruit, fill member slots and tell members which boosts to save. On this site the banner shows the upcoming battle's name and when it starts.

The opening days

Points pile up fastest early on, while everyone is excited about the new event. Big clans pull ahead, and smaller clans settle into the band of the leaderboard they can realistically hold.

The middle

Pace slows as casual players drop off. This is where steady clans gain ground: a clan where 40 of 50 members play a little every day often passes a clan whose score depends on a handful of grinders.

The final day

The last 24 hours decide most reward tiers. Clans hold back boosts and coordinated pushes for this window, and positions near tier borders can change several times in the final hour. Keep Compare or the Live Top 500 open to see every overtake as it happens.

Placement, medals and rewards

Rewards depend on the clan's final place when the timer hits zero. The exact tiers change from battle to battle, but recent battles have followed a similar pattern:

Final placeTypical reward
1stGold clan medal and the battle's main prize for members. In 2025 that was usually a Titanic pet.
2nd – 3rdSilver clan medal. Some battles extended silver further down, for example to 10th.
Next tiers (for example 4th – 10th, 11th – 50th)Bronze clan medal and huge pets, hoverboards or booths, depending on the tier.
Wider tiers (up to top 500 in recent battles)Participation rewards such as a clan gift.

Medals stay on the clan permanently and show how successful it has been over many battles. Always check the reward list of the current event in game, because BIG Games changes the tiers regularly.

Individual contribution

Inside a clan, every member's points are tracked separately. In recent battles the clan's top contributors received extra rewards, for example rainbow or golden versions of the main prize. Many clans also set their own rules, such as a minimum number of points to stay in the clan during a battle.

You can see every member's contribution on a clan profile, and compare yourself with players from all clans on the Top Players leaderboard.

Rules that affect clans

A few rules have been added over time to keep battles fair:

  • Kick limit during battles. To stop clans from cycling members in and out, clans can only kick a limited number of players per day while a battle runs (one per day at the time of writing).
  • Fair play. Some past battles had problems with macros and exploits. Breaking the game's rules can get accounts punished and clans removed from the leaderboard, and then every member loses the rewards. It's never worth the risk.
  • Points stay with the clan. Leaving mid-battle means your previous points stay behind and your new clan starts counting only what you earn after joining.

Following a battle on PS99 Clans

  1. Check the countdown and the order of the top clans on Top Clans.
  2. Open your clan's profile to see who is scoring and who hasn't started yet.
  3. Pick the clan directly above you in Compare and watch the gap over time.
  4. Check your own rank and hourly pace on Top Players.
  5. Streaming the battle? Add a live OBS overlay so viewers can follow along.

For a list of past battles and their winners, see the clan battle history.

Sources: in-game event descriptions and the community-maintained Pet Simulator Wiki. Reward tiers change between events. The in-game reward list always has the final say.
